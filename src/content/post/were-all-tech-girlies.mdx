In this story, the workshop is a co-op tucked into a sunlit warehouse. There are soldering irons next to weaving looms, a rack
of flight suits beside a pile of VR headsets. The "tech girlies" are everyone who wanders in and decides to stay for tea.
Gender is irrelevant; the nickname is just our shorthand for people who light up when a circuit closes or a CSS gradient
finally sings.

## Try each other's jackets on

Wednesday nights are swap meets. A firmware engineer teaches a choreographer how to calibrate sensors. The choreographer trades
a lesson on timing and flow. No one is trying to master everything; we're just borrowing each other's lenses long enough to see
new possibilities. We leave notes in the pockets for the next person who borrows the skill.

## Keep the tools out in the open

Every bench has instructions scribbled on butcher paper. The repo is projected on the wall so people can shout out comments.
There is no "junior" table. If you show up curious, someone will hand you the good screwdriver. The culture isn't performative
hype — it's care work disguised as enthusiasm.

## Celebrate maintenance like a runway show

On the first Friday of each month, we gather to show off repairs. Someone patched a decades-old drum machine. Someone else
refreshed a documentation site that future volunteers rely on. We clap for the invisible work, the way fashion lovers cheer for
a well-tailored hem. The applause echoes against the warehouse beams.

Maybe this workshop only exists in the liminal space between browser tabs. That's okay. The feeling is portable. Pack it with
you the next time you need proof that tech can be tender, communal, and kind.
